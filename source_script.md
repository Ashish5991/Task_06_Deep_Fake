## Working Script

This is synthetic audio generated for a research assignment.

In this research task, I examined how large language models respond when they are asked to reason about structured data rather than simply summarize text. The main lesson from the earlier analysis was that an AI model can sound confident even when its answer is incomplete or incorrect. For simple factual questions, such as identifying totals, averages, or the highest value in a small table, the model performed best when the data was clearly formatted and the question was specific. When the question required judgment, the answer became less reliable unless the metric was defined first.

The most important finding was that prompt design changes the quality of the response. A vague question encouraged the model to make assumptions. A stronger prompt, with definitions, instructions to show its reasoning, and a request to check calculations, produced answers that were easier to evaluate. However, even a well-written response still needed to be validated against ground-truth statistics. The model was useful as a reasoning assistant, but not as a final authority.

For a coach or researcher, this means AI can help organize questions, suggest possible interpretations, and explain patterns in accessible language. But the numerical claims must still be checked. The safest workflow is to compute the ground truth independently, ask the model to reason from that evidence, and then verify every major recommendation before acting on it.
